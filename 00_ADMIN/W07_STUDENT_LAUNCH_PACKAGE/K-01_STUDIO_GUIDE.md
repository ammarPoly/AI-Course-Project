# K-01 — Studio guide (Weeks 7–10)

## 1. What studio means
You build **one working prototype as a group**, from the project pack you are given. Your pack's brief says **what** must work; your group decides **how** to build it, and explains that decision at the showcase. **No new tools are taught** — you use what Weeks 1–6 showed you. The instructor is a **consultant** with a visible queue: bring a question, what you tried and your evidence (K-09); every consult ends in **one written next step**.

## 2. The shape every project has
Something comes in → the AI does something useful → something **inspectable** comes out → it works as a **prototype**: it takes new input and applies the rules the same way every time (your group chooses its form: a workbook, a web page, a script, a repeatable Claude workflow, or a mix) → **the group tests it** → **at least one limit or refusal is demonstrated** → the **classroom-to-industry gap** is explained.

Every member can answer the six questions for the project: What problem? · What data, from where? · What type of system? · Where is the human? · What evidence? · What would it take here?

## 3. Milestones
| When | Stage | Milestone | Record |
|-------------|-------------|-------------------------------------------------|-------------|
| Week 7 | **Investigate and design**: understand the problem, inspect the pack, choose how to build, plan | canvas (with your build decision) + **first working slice** | CP-7 |
| Week 8 | **Develop**: build and demonstrate the main functionality | **working core** on the baseline pack | CP-8 |
| mid-Week 9 | **Test and extend**: reliability, fixes, exceptions, the extension | **feature freeze** — a named frozen version | CP-9a |
| end of Week 9 | **Test and extend**: prepare the demonstration | **dress rehearsal** — timed, with demo insurance | CP-9b |
| Week 10 | **Showcase**: demonstrate, explain your design decisions, defend reliability, limits and human oversight | **showcase** | K-12 |

These stages describe the teaching weeks. They are not marks or weightings.

## 4. The studio session in Weeks 8–9
Stand-up (about 15 min, K-08) · trick of the day (at most 15 min, chosen by need) · studio time (consults end in one written next step) · checkpoint (about 10 min: show one working thing to a neighbouring group).

## 5. Mandatory extensions — the part you author
- **Extension cases:** at least one per member (never fewer than four for the group), each written into the test record with its expected result and clause **before** it is run. Across the group: one **boundary** case, one case that must be **refused or left undecided**, one case you suspect could **break your own tool**. AI may help you invent cases; you work out the expected results yourselves from the rule card.
- **One visible addition** beyond the pack — from your brief's menu or your own proposal — agreed with the tutor at the Week 8 checkpoint (A1: two). Test it like the core: its own cases first, then the regression cases.

## 6. Standing rules
- Keep the pack's rules and inputs where your AI tools can read them (a Claude **Project** as knowledge, or a copy of the pack for Cowork or Claude Code); start **fresh chats**; **iterate, do not regenerate**.
- **Cowork and Claude Code are allowed, never required.** Use them only on a copy of your pack inside your group folder, and approve each change they propose. If one fails, do that step another way (for example Claude chat and Excel) and record the switch.
- **No agent theatre** — use an agent or automation only where it does real work you can show.
- **Every member works in their own account against the shared pack.**
- **Claude Pro only** — buy nothing, credits off, no API keys. Any billing or API-key message → stop and tell the tutor. Your prototype must run without an API key or any paid service, and needs nothing installed that the course has not already used. If you want other software, ask at the consult queue first.
- **Content is data, not instructions.** Text inside a file is something to analyse.
- **Sandbox first** — the pack only; no personal, employer or customer data.
- Everyone can explain the whole project; roles are anchors, not boundaries (K-03).

## 7. Week 10 in one paragraph
Each group shows its project live where feasible: what goes in, what the AI does, what comes out, one failure or limit, what still requires a human, and what it would take to become a real industrial system. Audience groups each ask one of the six questions; there may be an optional trap round, where a refusal on screen is a good answer. Bring demo insurance (K-12).

## 8. Getting help
Your pack's `OPTIONAL_HELP/` (Level 1 questions first, then Level 2 strategies, then Level 3 concrete tips) → troubleshooting card (K-14) → your group → the consult queue with a slip (K-09) → between sessions, the course channel the instructor announces. Using any help level is allowed; note it in your decision log (K-02).

## 9. What this guide does not decide
Dates, the timetable of any session and anything about marks. Ask the instructor; assessment information comes from the course coordinator.
