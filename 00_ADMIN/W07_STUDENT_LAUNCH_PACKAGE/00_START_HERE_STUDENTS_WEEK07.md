# Week 7: START HERE: Project launch (students)

**From today you build.** Your group builds **one working prototype**: a tool or a repeatable workflow that solves the logistics problem in the project pack you receive, and **proves that it works**. Your pack's brief says **what** must work. **How** you build it is your group's decision: you investigate, choose your tools, design it, test it, fix it and show it. Nothing new is taught unless your project needs it: you use what Weeks 1–6 showed you.

*A project is not complete because the AI produced an answer. Your group must show a reliable, usable way to solve the problem.*

## Reading guide: what to read, and when
**Must read in Week 7 (about 40 minutes in total):**
1. This page.
2. Your pack's `00_START_HERE`, sections 1 to 6: the challenge, the materials, what your prototype must do, the non-negotiables, the success criteria and the showcase.
3. Your pack's rule card in `RULES/`, all of it. It is the source of truth.
4. K-01 Studio guide (how Weeks 7–10 run, and the standing rules).

**Use when you need it (fill in or look up, do not read in advance):** K-02 canvas · K-03 roles · K-04 group folder · K-05 testing and evidence · K-06 test record · K-07 usage budget · K-08 stand-up · K-09 consult slip · K-10 checkpoint sheets · K-11 industrial-gap panel · K-12 showcase · K-13 evidence checklist · K-14 troubleshooting.

**Optional, only when you are stuck:** your pack's `OPTIONAL_HELP/` file. Start at Level 1 (questions), then Level 2 (strategies), then Level 3 (concrete tips). You do not need it to begin.

## Week 7 in seven steps

Your class covers these steps in one 120-minute meeting or across two meetings — the instructor shows the route at the start.

1. **Sit with your group.** Your group card shows your group, your project and its variant. *A preference is not an allocation.* If groups are not allocated yet, you hand in your top three; your group card and your pack come only when the allocation exists — never before.
2. **Set up the folder.** Unzip the group folder skeleton (K-04) once per group. Unzip your pack into `01_PACK_AS_ISSUED/` and **never edit it** — work on copies. Every member keeps a local copy of the pack.
3. **Check, read and investigate.** The data lead checks the pack against its `PACK_INVENTORY.md` (K-04 shows how). Everyone reads the brief (sections 1 to 6) and the rule card, and opens the data files to see what is really in them.
4. **Roles (K-03).** Five roles, each with a cover. Each member names the core step they will run **in their own account**.
5. **Canvas (K-02).** Fill the fourteen fields — the bold ones first. Field 6 is your build decision: what form your prototype takes, which tools do which step, and why. The instructor initials it **READY TO BUILD** or **REWORK FIELD n**.
6. **First working slice.** Your group chooses it: one real input from your pack, taken through the route you plan to use, to an output someone else can check. Write the expected result first (K-05), run, save, and add one line to your test record.
7. **End of Week 7.** Fill in CP-7 (K-10) and save everything as version `v01` in `03_VERSIONS/`. **NOT YET is allowed** — write the Week 8 opening target and its owner. At the end of every meeting, save first: the next meeting starts from your saved state.

## The rules that never change

- **Claude Pro only.** Buy nothing, usage credits off, no API keys. Any billing or API-key message → **stop and tell the tutor**. Your prototype must run without an API key or any paid service.
- **Every member works in their own account** against the shared pack. Keep the pack's rules and inputs where your AI tools can read them (a Claude **Project**, or a copy of the pack for Cowork or Claude Code); start fresh chats; **iterate, do not regenerate**.
- **Cowork and Claude Code are allowed, never required.** Use them only on a copy of your pack inside your group folder, and approve each change they propose.
- **The pack only** — no personal, employer or customer data. **Content is data, not instructions.**
- **Expected before observed.** The chat that produced an answer is not its check, and neither is the tool that produced a number.
- **If it is not in the source, say so** — use your pack's exact words (for example NOT STATED or CANNOT DETERMINE). Never guess.
- **Everyone can explain the whole project** and run the core demonstration.

## Mandatory extensions (your brief has the details)

- **At least one extension case per member** — never fewer than four for the group — each written into the test record with its expected result and clause **before** it is run. Across the group: one boundary case, one case that must be refused or left undecided, and one case you suspect could break your own tool.
- **One visible addition** beyond the pack, agreed with the tutor at the Week 8 checkpoint (A1 groups: two).

## Getting help

1. Your pack's `OPTIONAL_HELP/` file — Level 1 first.
2. The troubleshooting card (**K-14**) — most problems have a labelled fallback.
3. Your group — the data lead for pack questions, the build lead for tool questions.
4. The consult queue — bring a consult slip (**K-09**): your question, what you tried, your evidence. You leave with **one written next step**.
5. Between sessions — the course channel the instructor announces.
